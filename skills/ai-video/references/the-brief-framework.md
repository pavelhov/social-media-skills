# BRIEF — a portable story and production handoff

A portable creative brief describes what the audience should see and hear. Keep the authorized
provider, billing route and supported control mapping in a separate execution section. Portability
does not authorize switching tools, billing or assembly methods.

## B — Brief the story and each local shot

Read the selected identity's brand, voice, story system, production defaults and continuity ledger
when available. Preserve its novelty, cast, caption/dialogue, runtime, route and delivery choices.
A generative story first needs **short-form-video-script** and **hook-writer**: desire → action →
consequence → visible payoff, timed beat sheet and distinct executable openings. Fresh premise work
belongs to **idea-generation-and-ideation** or **content-research-and-sourcing** as needed.

Review the actual payoff board and required late cast before any opening motion, with story revision,
asset hashes, named predicates and pass/fail/unknown evidence. A cold reader should be able to explain
the causal chain from the boards without the author's pitch. Record an actual response, or mark the
review pending. A text plan does not prove image correctness.

For each shot, plan local start and completed-end boards and specify its own initial state, dominant
action and completed end state. Reviewed end-state planning does not require every provider request
to use endpoint pinning; distinguish the plan from the controls required by the approved method. Add named cast,
exact speakers, prop/body invariants and permitted transformations. “Starts to enter” is insufficient
when the next scene requires being inside. Build ambitious physical action through staging, time and
coverage, not contradictory instructions to move dramatically while staying perfectly still.

## R — Route the approved job by fit

Match output category (generative story/B-roll, avatar, voiceover, clipping) to available tools. Check
the installed authorized route for controls, duration, audio and output support. Hand provider prompt
craft to **veo-3**, **grok-imagine**, **kling**, **luma**, **runway**, **heygen**, **synthesia** or the relevant
audio/editing skill. For Grok continuity planning, read the sibling
[shot continuity and control-role guide](../../grok-imagine/references/shot-continuity-and-control-roles.md).
Current provider details belong to their integration owners; the canonical Grok guide is
`tools/integrations/grok-imagine.md` in a source checkout. Installed skill collections may omit that
repository directory: verify capabilities and receipts against the installed authorized adapter.
API support or prices do not establish CLI support or media cost.
If a required control is unsupported, block and present the limitation; do not silently drop it or
switch the route. Film actual proof/product/location when truthful representation requires it.

## I — Inspect inputs before authorized generation

Plan cheap checks first: script read-through, causal-board review, geometry, cast and input-role review.
Image or video tests still require their applicable approval; do not run several providers as a hidden
“test.” Show premise, beat sheet, target runtime, clip count, cast, route/method, first-pass calls and
estimated known/unknown cost for explicit batch approval before spending. Follow stricter identity
requirements. The allowance covers only its disclosed scope; corrective rerolls need exact repair
scope and attempt approval. Preserve originals and selected successes.

Required cast identity, completed action, speaker/source, possession and payoff checks are critical.
Rejected, unknown, missing or stale evidence blocks motion, irrespective of budget or review rounds.
A wrong cast, extra creature or impossible contact in a still cannot be fixed by negative wording in
the video prompt. Revise/review that still under the applicable image authorization first.

## E — Edit with deliberate continuity and cuts

Choose coverage to make the action and consequence read. A provider clip is not automatically a story
beat or edited shot. Derive runtime from action and natural dialogue; clip count follows the approved
beats and verified limits, with no fixed duration/clip quota. Respect a user-locked one-shot plan; where
unlocked, propose motivated multi-shot coverage when it improves clarity.

Use `continuous`, `hard_cut`, `match_cut` or `transformation` explicitly. State what changes
and what persists. Keep the physical action's completed endpoint separate from the following shot's
composition; do not force an exterior entry and a new interior location into one interpolation. For
continuity, bind the selected upstream attempt/output hash and observed outgoing frame/action review.
Changing that selection or bytes requires fresh dependent review. Identity references are design
anchors, not evidence the preceding clip actually completed its action.

Assembly follows the approved method and identity defaults. Do not add trimming, captions, music or
provider changes by habit. Existing-footage edits may be the smallest repair; apply the relevant scope
rules. Composed footage remains a review draft until selected picture and synchronized sound pass.

## F — Finalize with evidence

Separate transport success, technical export, visual, audio, story and release status. Full audiovisual
claims require complete synchronized playback of the selected master, coverage and matching output
hashes; sampled contact sheets or ASR alone cannot establish exact speakers, dialogue or action timing.
Missing/failed critical evidence remains draft. Offline fixtures and written plans do not prove live
visual reliability, and neither proves reach. Plan appropriate disclosure and explicit publishing
confirmation through **scheduling-and-queue → WoopSocial**, which only schedules/publishes.
